# PLAN-1000 — the step and the bytes

Scope: step time and bytes only, for 1000 tok/s single stream, same weights, exact block rejection
(no FLy), T=1.0, served config r21 / DSpark k=5 / 6 verified rows / TP4 on 4× RTX PRO 6000 SE.

Labels on every number: **[them]** measured by the Moet/vLLM/DSpark teams and recorded in the local
clone or a public source; **[us]** measured locally in `work/`; **[me]** my estimate from measured
inputs, arithmetic shown.

length (in tokens) equals step time (in ms)**. Today AL = 3.45 and step = 9.1 ms → 377 tok/s [us].

**Sum of 1+4+5+6+7+8+9+10 (no dense-quant risk) = −3.22 ms → 5.88 ms.** At today's AL 3.45 that is
587 tok/s. With lever 2 instead of 1 (the low-risk pair): −2.45 ms → 6.65 ms → 519 tok/s.

**Highest expected value: lever 1, the 2-bit routed experts.** It is the only lever worth >1 ms, it is
3× the next item, and it does something the others do not: it frees ~34 GiB/GPU (routed experts
290.2 GB checkpoint → 145 GB, `work/RESULTS.md:26`; per-GPU weights 73.62 GiB → ~40 GiB, of which
6.0 GiB is non-expert), which pays for the FP4 delta pool that makes the quality argument work *and*
lifts the KV pool from 4.19M tokens to ~45M at the measured 794 B/token. Nothing else in this list
buys capacity.

**Highest certainty: lever 4.** Zero quality risk, zero numerics risk, 4 days, and the patch is
already designed and half-built (`P2P_AR_FUSED_MAX_ROWS`, `work/STATUS.md:114`).

## 2-bit experts

### The recipe (exactly what the repo does)

Three layers, all in `kernels/MANIFEST.md:57-75` and `docs/v024-port.md:60-90`:

1. **Base plane.** Routed experts go from FP4 (e2m1, `int8 [2304,2560]` two nibbles/byte) to a 2-bit
   *sign-symmetric* codebook `{−4,−1,1,4}` on the same 32×32 UE8M0 block grid — same container, same
   loader, same scale layout, half the bytes. 33,023 of 33,024 tensors pick the same codebook [them]
   (`docs/quality.md:34`).
2. **Refinement plane.** `moe_w4q_mm` (split FP4, bit-exact): a radix-5 "quintal" plane at
   2.5 bits/elem on top of the 2-bit base reconstructs all 16 e2m1 nibbles exactly, zeros included.
   Slot = 5/8 of the non-split nibble plane → **1.6× experts/GiB**, kernel cost +11 % at K=4096,
   +6 % at K=2048/512 [them] (`kernels/MANIFEST.md:57`). Bit-exact means it cannot change quality —
   it only changes bytes.
3. **FP4 delta pool** (`VLLM_MOE_W2_DELTA_GB`). A runtime-chosen hot set of experts kept at full FP4
   and force-promoted on low-confidence decode steps (`VLLM_MOE_W2_GATE`). 170 hot-expert slots ≈
   2 GiB [them] (`docs/quality.md:42-46`). `=auto` sizes it from free VRAM after the KV cache is
   allocated (`docs/v024-port.md:234`).

### The speed

- 1-GPU PRO 6000, MTP k=2: **151 tok/s** on 2-bit base alone, **143 tok/s** with the FP4 delta tier
  (≈ −0.6 ms/step for the delta) [them] (`docs/quality.md:44`).
- That 151 tok/s is *not* the byte saving — it is the 2-bit line serving a 159B model that does not fit
  otherwise. The transferable number for us is the byte cut: 4.23 → 2.1 GB/rank/step [us] at
  1.41–1.45 TB/s achieved → **−1.47 to −1.51 ms** [me]. `work/STATUS.md:696` quotes −1.8 ms; I keep
  the conservative figure because the marginal-row measurement implies the MoE already runs slightly
  above 1.45 TB/s on short bursts.
- Capacity: routed experts are 290.2 GB of the 510.3 GB checkpoint (`work/RESULTS.md:26`). At 2-bit
  that is 145 GB, i.e. per-GPU weights 73.62 GiB → ~40 GiB, freeing **~34 GiB/GPU** [me] — at the
  measured 794 B/token that is ~45M KV tokens, or a 4 GiB delta pool *and* ~40M KV tokens. Both free.

### The quality evidence — and what actually decided it

**Local, on the real model, same serving stack, only the expert codes changed** [them]
(`docs/quality.md:20-28`):

| codebook | bits | MTP acc. length | draft accept % | arithmetic | coherence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| FP4 (official) | 4.0 | 2.68 | 84.1 | 3/5 | 12/12 |
| K=8 (3-bit) | 3.0 | 2.60 | 79.8 | 3/5 | 12/12 |
| K=6 (2.58-bit) | 2.58 | 2.68 | 84.0 | 2/5 | 11/12 |
| K=4 naive 2-bit, asymmetric | 2.0 | **1.00** | **0.0** | 0/5 | **0/12 broken** |
| **K=4 tensor-sym `{−4,−1,1,4}`** | 2.0 | **2.73** | **86.3** | 2/5 | **12/12** |

The decisive finding: **naive 2-bit collapses to a degenerate loop, and the cause is sign bias, not
magnitude.** The optimal-L2 2-bit codebook is sign-asymmetric; accumulated per-expert sign bias over
43 layers destroys the model. Forcing a sign-symmetric codebook at the *same* L2 error restores
acceptance to at/above the official FP4 experts (2.73/86.3 vs 2.68/84.1), 12/12 coherent. Symmetry,
not granularity, is the lever. Production 1-GPU A/B: **2.725 / 2.744** with the delta tier vs the
same-run two-card FP4 control **2.546 / 2.496** (`docs/quality.md:49`).

Weight-space error on our own weights (`work/quant/re-quant2.py`, expert 0, layer 6, w1 and w3, the
same 32×32 grid and the fp4 dequant) [us]:

| | uniform 2-bit | uniform 3-bit | per-block Lloyd 2-bit | Lloyd 3-bit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| w1 rel-RMS vs the FP4 reconstruction | 40.26 % | 20.03 % | 31.47 % | 16.61 % |
| w3 | 40.29 % | 20.05 % | 31.49 % | 16.62 % |

Published, for the "how much expert error does a DeepSeek-class MoE tolerate" question:

- **PagedMoE** ([2611.03173](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03173)) — routed-output relative error on a
  397B-A17B MoE: uniform 4-bit **15.3 %**, uniform 3-bit **29.6 %**, uniform 2-bit **54.7 %**; a
  routing-weight-driven allocator at the same average budget brings 2.5-bit average down to
  **36.8 %** and keeps GSM8K/GPQA/etc. close to GPTQ-INT4 and MXFP4. Their conclusion is the one that
  matters for us: *"the quality cost of a 2.5-bit average budget remains model- and task-dependent."*
- **RotaryQuant** ([2608.08081](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08081)) — ships 4-bit dense / **2-bit routed
  experts** / 8-bit shared expert on a 26–120B MoE, on the explicit grounds that the shared expert's
  activation kurtosis resists compression. That is the precedent for lever 3's risk asymmetry and for
  keeping the shared expert out of the dense-quant cut.
- **vLLM's own 2-bit line** treats bare 2-bit as unfit to serve DS4: *"Supported DS4 recipes must
  retain a positive FP4 recovery pool"* (`docs/v024-port.md:229`).

### The delta pool, concretely

The delta tier is not optional for us. Bare 2-bit is "coherent but numerically sloppy": the
`17×23−100` probe fails without it and returns the exact 291 with it (`docs/quality.md:42-46`). At
40 % weight-RMS error the model still writes fluent text and still passes coherence, but multi-step
arithmetic degrades — which for a max-effort thinking agent workload is the whole product.

Sizing for us: at 2-bit we free ~34 GiB/GPU. A 4 GiB FP4 delta pool per rank = 4 GiB / 4.7 MB ≈
**~914 hot expert slots per rank** out of 3,552 (37 layers × 96 experts) = **26 % coverage** [me]. The
repo's 170-slot / 2 GiB pool was enough to close the arithmetic gap on a 159B model; 26 % coverage is
5× that. Take `VLLM_MOE_W2_DELTA_GB=auto` with `VLLM_MOE_W2_DELTA_RESERVE_GB` set for our graph
capture headroom (we run 128 graph sizes and 4.19M KV tokens, so the reserve is larger than the
default 3).

### The recommended quality gate (this is the decision, not the speed)

Two-stage, and the gate is on the *delta-pool-on* config, never on bare 2-bit:

**Stage 1 — screen (hours, one test VM boot).** `bench/runner/bench.py --suite quality` against the
committed r21 native baseline, schema-2, identical serving geometry (TP4, k=5, 8 seqs, 8192 chunk,
graphs 128):
- GSM8K-200 paired exact McNemar. Pass = flips ≤ 2/2 and Δpp inside the measured noise floor
  (`docs/dsv41-sm120-vs-0xsero.md` "Quality: parity": the same stack against itself flips 1/1, two
  independent 4-bit runs 2 items apart).
- **Completion-token inflation ≤ +2 %.** The repo treats +8–11 % as a real quality bug that accuracy
  alone did not catch (`bench/README.md:140-150`); it is the earliest damage signal we have.
- MTP acceptance within ±0.05 of 3.42–3.52 — the stated noise band (`docs/quality.md:16`).

**Stage 2 — release gate (hours of GPU).** GPQA-diamond non-think paired per-item median / geometric-
mean token ratios + exact sign test; GPQA THINK; needle PASS at 100K; the 12-prompt coherence and
5-problem arithmetic sets; plus a KLD run against the r21 baseline on a held-out set (the rtx6kpro
wiki's methodology, `local-inference-lab/rtx6kpro`).

**Kill criteria, written down before the run:** any completion-token inflation > +2 %, any GSM8K flip
asymmetry > 3 items, MTP acceptance below 3.35, or a single needle miss at 100K → ship **3-bit**
(lever 2, −0.73 ms, 16.6 % rel-RMS, near-zero risk) and revisit.

## The non-MoE 6.1 ms

### (a) mHC boundary path — 1.07 ms, roofline 0.37 ms → take 0.15 (ceiling 0.46)

The path is 80 boundaries × 13.4 µs = PCIe push + fused kernel. The push is already its own launch:
`tools/dsv41_sm120/README.md:35` measures **per boundary push 5.7 + fused kernel 7.7 µs instead of
NCCL 11.8 + `post_norm` 3.9**, and the fused consumer is the served path
(`VLLM_MOE_P2P_AR_FUSED_MAX_ROWS`). `work/STATUS.md:114` prices the remaining lever — issuing the push
from the producing kernel — at **−0.12…−0.20 ms over 2–4 days**; `work/leads/LEADS.md` L5 quotes
−0.46 ms, which is 5.7 µs × 80, i.e. it assumes the push becomes entirely free. It cannot: the bytes
still cross PCIe (420 KB per boundary on the fused path, `work/STATUS.md:111`). **Use −0.15 ms.**

Recipe: in `wo_b` / the MoE epilogue, after the last store, issue the same `st.release.sys` push to
peer `(rank+1+p) % 4` and bump the sequence counter from the kernel's last CTA — no new launch, no new
ordering rule. **The platform constraint is already measured and must be respected: one GPU writes to
one peer at a time; a kernel storing to two or more peers concurrently runs ~16× slower (90–113 µs for
3 × 60 KB)** [them] (`tools/dsv41_sm120/README.md:35`). Bit-identity is free — the push writes the
bytes the launch wrote.

### (b) lm_head + vocab all-gather + SWA builders inside the FULL graph — 0.22

- Target lm_head 0.11–0.14 ms and the vocab all-gather sit outside every graph. The all-gather is
  **54.6 vs 62.1 µs** for `[6, 32320]` on the P2P push [them] (`tools/dsv41_sm120/README.md:35`,
  step 25). Both are graph-capturable as-is.
- The SWA metadata builders are the bigger miss: `build()` runs **outside** the graphs, 24 launches of
  1–2 µs plus gaps, ~30 µs of kernel time; step 32's single-launch kernel takes it **175.9 → 100.3 µs**
  eager [them] (`tools/dsv41_sm120/README.md:42`). Capturing it removes the host-side gaps entirely.
- The structural point: a kernel inside a CUDA graph cannot take less than ~2 µs, so *anything* left
  outside a graph pays the eager launch path (`docs/dsv41-sm120-port.md:301`). Our eager tail is 0.4 ms
  and the sampler 0.3 ms for exactly this reason. The recipe is mechanical: extend the FULL graph's
  capture range over the lm_head → all-gather → sampler chain and the eight SWA builders, with
  `capture_error_mode="thread_local"` (required on sm_120, `docs/v024-port.md` SM120 fix 4).

### (c) Launch gaps — 0.53 ms of ~1500 kernels × 0.35 µs → take 0.30

Most of this is already harvested (NCCL graph records −0.2…−0.35 ms, GEMV PDL −0.15…−0.35,
FFN-boundary PDL −0.075…−0.1, FC2 discard −0.15…−0.28 — all measured, all served,
`docs/dsv41-sm120-port.md:1257-1364`). What is left:

- **Fuse the three `mxfp8_quantize` launches per layer into the GEMV prologue.** This is the repo's own
  named remaining lever: *"the remaining lever is launch count (fusing the three `mxfp8_quantize`
  launches per layer into the GEMV prologue)"* (`tools/dsv41_sm120/README.md:78-79`, and
  `docs/dsv41-sm120-port.md:301` "−185 launches"). 3 × 40 layers = 120 launches.
- PDL the remaining plain-launched kernels; `moe_quant_scatter` and `spread` already are.

**Do not reach for conditional nodes or device graph launch here.** CUDA 12.8+ conditional nodes
([NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/dynamic-control-flow-in-cuda-graphs-with-conditional-nodes))
buy *dynamic* control flow inside one graph. Our served graph is static (fixed k = 5, 6 rows, 128 graph
sizes) and the only dynamic-knob candidate — adaptive verification — measured **−14 %** on code single
stream [them] (`docs/dsv41-sm120-port.md:355-370`). A conditional node with no live branch is a
megakernel by another name, and megakernels lost here: step 37's full MoE sublayer fold measured
**132.3 µs vs 128.9 µs in situ, critical path +227 µs, +2.7 %** [them]
(`tools/dsv41_sm120/README.md:47`). The reason is in the same line: *the phases are serial, so the
fusion saves only the two launch gaps (~1 µs) while it loses the spread's PDL overlap with the
preceding boundary kernel (~3 µs)*. Published megakernel work agrees that the win is in the scheduler,
not the fusion ([2604.13327](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13327v2)).

### (d) The replicated q_a / kv_a / main_proj traffic — 0.10

q_a and kv_a are replicated on every rank (the brief). At TP4 each rank reads the full 1280×5120 and
576×5120 slabs: 13.1 MB + 5.9 MB = 19 MB/rank/step, and the drafter's `main_proj` is 78 MB replicated
[us, brief]. 97 MB/rank/step ÷ 1.45 TB/s = **0.07 ms** [me] — the whole item is small. Sharding them
needs a collective to reassemble, which costs more than it saves at 6 rows. **Take only the `main_proj`
half** (shard to 19.5 MB/rank, −0.04 ms) and count this lever as ~0.05–0.10, not as a headline.

### (e) Sparse MLA at 3× its byte floor — 0.37 → 0.15 realistic

Already at **7.07 µs decode + 2.1 µs merge per layer** after the `ldmatrix` XV rewrite (7.94 → 7.07 in
the served graph, −29 µs/step) [them] (`tools/dsv41_sm120/README.md:39`). The byte floor is ~0.12 ms
because the gather is a scatter of 32-token pages, not a stream. Remaining recipe, in the order the
repo itself ranks it: the fused decode kernel from the cubit repo already written for the V4 page
geometry (2.5× Triton) needs re-targeting to the V4.1 layout (SWA pages of 32, compressed 128/64) and
FlashInfer's dispatch — `docs/dsv41-sm120-port.md:339` calls it a multi-day effort for ≤0.5 ms.
**Take −0.15.**

## Dead ends

| # | dead end | the number | source |

| 1 | **L2-resident expert caching across decode steps** | Between visiting layer *i* at step *t* and step *t+1* you stream 36 other layers × ~106 MB = **3.8 GB** through a **128 MB** L2 (GB202 full die, [NVIDIA arch PDF](https://www.nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/design-visualization/quadro-product-literature/NVIDIA-RTX-Blackwell-PRO-GPU-Architecture-v1.0.pdf)). ## Card level: is 1.45 TB/s the ceiling?

**Yes, 1.45 TB/s is 91 % of the part's 1,597 GB/s peak, and I found no measured trick that raises it.**

- Peak is **1,597 GB/s at 12,481 MHz** on a 512-bit GDDR7 bus (PNY RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell **Server
  Edition** product brief, p. 2-3). 1.45/1.597 = **90.8 %** — already at the top of what a streaming
  read achieves on GDDR7.
- **The reference host's 25–29 % advantage is the card, not the recipe.** The *Workstation* RTX PRO
  6000 is rated **1.79 TB/s** (BIZON/techpowerup spec tables) — 12 % more peak from the higher memory
  clock — and the Moet host adds a +5000 MHz afterburner on top. The Server Edition's memory clock is
  fixed at 12,481 MHz (the brief), so that 25–29 % is **not recoverable on this part**.
- L2 access policy windows (`cudaStreamSetAttribute` + `cudaAccessPolicyWindow`, CUDA 11.0+,
  [CUDA guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-programming-guide/04-special-topics/l2-cache-control.html))
  cannot help a 4.23 GB stream through a 128 MB L2; `hitRatio` is a hint, not a guarantee, and the
  reserved set-aside can only shrink the streaming path. **1 day to falsify, predicted null.** The one
  place it could pay is the dense 1.8 GB re-read — also ≫ 128 MB. Not worth the day unless the bundle
  is already built.
- Already harvested and measured: `NCCL_P2P_LEVEL=SYS` (small all-reduces 18–30 → ~12 µs), NCCL graph
  event records (−0.2…−0.35 ms), PDL everywhere it pays, `L1::no_allocate` + L2 evict-first on the GEMV
  weights (48.4 → 38.6 µs per attention layer), FC2 padding-row `discard.global.L2` (−3.8…−7.0 µs per
  MoE layer), power 325 → 400 W (+7 % steps/s on the dev cards; 450 W tripped breakers).
- The real remaining card-level number is the **mHC push, which is on the PCIe egress floor**
  (`docs/dsv41-sm120-port.md:1915`), not on DRAM.
