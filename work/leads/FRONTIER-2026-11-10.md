# Frontier survey: raising single-stream decode tok/s on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (4x RTX PRO 6000, vLLM TP4)

Date: 2026-11-10. Scope: read-only literature/web survey (arXiv API, GitHub, HuggingFace, vendor blogs; r.jina.ai where needed). Every number below is quoted or paraphrased from the cited primary source; derived arithmetic is labeled DERIVED and is not a measurement. Unverifiable claims are marked UNVERIFIED.

Stack context as given: 502B-param MoE ("DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017-FP8-abliterated", 40 layers, 384 routed experts top-6 + 1 shared, FP4 experts + FP8 dense, MLA + sparse indexer, native MTP block k=5), vLLM, TP=4 over PCIe (no NVLink), 4x RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server (SM120, 96 GB, 1.45 TB/s each).
Note on identity: the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash paper (arXiv 2609.19969) reports "552B backbone parameters... activates 16B parameters per token during decode but only 8B during prefill" with a Causal Encoder-Decoder architecture; V4-Flash (arXiv 2606.19348) is 284B/13B-active. The -1017 checkpoint is a later revision (arXiv 2610.35671: "departs from V4 with a new architecture"). Your 502B/40-layer spec is treated as authoritative for your checkpoint.

## 1. Speculative decoding / MTP (accepted length at k>=5, temperature 1.0)

- The 2026 state of the art for DeepSeek-class targets is the DSpark/DFlash line, not vanilla MTP. DSpark (arXiv 2607.05147, DeepSeek, 2026-07): "a semi-autoregressive architecture, coupling a parallel backbone with a lightweight sequential module" plus "confidence-scheduled verification, dynamically tailoring the verification length for each request based on estimated prefix survival probabilities". Deployed: "Compared to the established production baseline (MTP-1), DSpark accelerates per-user generation speeds by 60 to 85 percent at matched throughput levels." Blog restates: "57-85% more per-user speed on the exact same hardware, with identical outputs."
- DFlash (arXiv 2602.06036): "generating draft tokens in a single forward pass... achieves over 6x lossless acceleration... up to 2.5x higher speedup than the state-of-the-art speculative decoding method EAGLE-3." Official training code + checkpoints: github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSpec (DSpark/DFlash/Eagle3, block-7 configs; 38TB target cache warning for Qwen3-4B-scale training) and github.com/z-lab/dflash (DFlash2: "DFlash 2 collection" on HF; serving in SGLang PR 35371, vLLM PR 52816, llama.cpp PR 27342). DFlash2 has no standalone arXiv paper found as of 2026-11-10; the repo/blog + third-party evals (arXiv 2611.10432, 2611.00588, 2610.13708) are primary.
- A ready DFlash speculator exists for exactly deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Flash: huggingface.co/RedHatAI/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-speculator.dflash - 5-layer all-SWA draft, hc_mult=4 hidden-state streams, "predicts up to 7 speculative tokens", greedy per-position acceptance 78.8%/58.8%/45.4%/35.9%/29.0%/23.6%/19.3%, "Full-sequence acceptance 41.6%", "Approx. mean accepted length ~3.9 tokens". Serves via vLLM `--speculative-config {"model": "...", "num_speculative_tokens": 7, "method": "dflash"}`. Caveat: trained on the stock model; your FP8-abliterated target will shift acceptance; retrain with vllm-project/speculators (same pipeline, "trained using the Speculators library").
- Longer blocks / past native MTP length: nobody retrains the native k=5 MTP head; 2026 work swaps in external block drafters trained at block 7-8 (DeepSpec `dspark_block7_*` configs; DFlash block_size 8). D-REAL (arXiv 2611.10432) pushes acceptance length further via post-training: "+up to 0.23 tokens... under greedy (T=0.0) and stochastic (T=1.0) sampling, translating into up to a 9.4% end-to-end serving throughput improvement" on DSpark and DFlash2. TVP (arXiv 2611.00588): acceptance-matched training objectives improve "+6.2 to 12.9%" over public DFlash2 recipes.
- Temperature 1.0: DSpine (arXiv 2609.36173) reports "the longest acceptance length at both temperatures" (Qwen3-4B/8B; mean 4.82 vs DFlash 3.77 at T=0, +23.3% SGLang throughput). D-REAL explicitly measures T=1.0 gains. SpecTr (arXiv 2310.15141) was designed for the sampling (high-temperature) regime.
- Tree drafts on V4 (arXiv 2609.24698): branch-aware causal verification consistent with CSA/HCA states; "at D=8 about 2.83-3.41 versus 2.39-2.84" accepted, "up to about 18.5%" throughput gain across bs 1-64 on DeepSeek-V4-Flash.
- Classics for completeness: EAGLE-3 (2503.01840) "up to 6.5x"; Medusa-2 "2.3-3.6x"; Hydra++ "up to 1.31x" over Medusa; REST "1.62X to 2.36X"; Lookahead "up to 1.8x on MT-Bench". EAGLE-4: no arXiv paper found (searched title/abstract; no result).

## 2. Exact (lossless) verification

- Block Verification (arXiv 2403.10444): "verifies the entire block jointly... optimal in the expected number of tokens produced each iteration and specifically is never worse than the standard token-level verification. Empirically... 5%-8% in a range of tasks and datasets." This is the direct answer to "block rejection at k=5": published joint-block verification adds 5-8% over naive token-by-token, keeping the identical-distribution guarantee.
- >10% at k=5 with no distribution change: no published single-draft method found. The >10% class is multi-draft: SpecTr (2310.15141) k-candidates per token via optimal transport, "wall clock speedup of 2.13X, a further 1.37X speedup over speculative decoding"; SpecHub (2411.05289) "0.05-0.27 and 0.02-0.16 more tokens per step than RRS and RRS without replacement" at linear overhead; Towards Optimal Multi-draft (2502.18779) proves existing verifiers sit below the theoretical acceptance bound; Global Resolution (2511.15898, 2025-11) gives "the first multi-draft algorithm with 90% acceptance and under 100 ms of overhead per generated token with negligible deviation from the target model distribution".
- Token-tree verification originates with SpecInfer (2305.09781): "verified against the LLM in parallel using a novel tree-based parallel decoding mechanism". Tree verify is exactly lossless under greedy; under sampling it needs the generalized rejection rules above.
- Assessment for us: adopt Block Verification (cheap, safe); treat multi-draft OT as a research lever only if draft-side cost per step can be amortized on PCIe TP4.

## 3. MoE decode kernels on Blackwell / SM120

- FlashInfer (github.com/flashinfer-ai/flashinfer): "Support for SM75 (Turing) and later (through Blackwell)"; table lists "SM 12.0, 12.1 - RTX 50 series, DGX Spark"; "[2025-10-08] Blackwell support added in v0.4.0"; "FP4 GEMM: NVFP4 and MXFP4 matrix multiplication for Blackwell GPUs"; "Quantized MoE: FP8 and FP4 expert weights with block-wise scaling"; "Grouped GEMM... for LoRA and multi-expert routing"; "AllReduce: Custom implementations". This is your SM120-native kernel base.
- DeepGEMM (github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepGEMM): now "GEMMs (FP8, FP4, BF16), fused MoE with overlapped communication (Mega MoE), MQA scoring for the lightning indexer, HyperConnection (HC)"; changelog 2026.04.16 "Mega MoE, FP8xFP4 GEMM, FP4 Indexer, PDL", 2026.09.10 "Sparse Indexer, Mega Gate, Mega mHC"; "Mega MoE fuses and overlaps EP dispatch, linear 1 and linear 2 (FP8xFP4 or FP8xFP8), SwiGLU, and EP combine into a single mega-kernel, overlapping NVLink communication and tensor core computation. It requires multi-process launch with symmetric memory." Blocker for us: README requires "NVIDIA SM90 or SM100 architecture GPU" - SM120 (RTX PRO 6000) is not supported; and Mega MoE assumes NVLink EP, not PCIe TP.
- DeepInfer (github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer): DeepSeek's own open engine ("the inference engine behind its own service"), source of the Mega kernels (MegaAttention, MegaMoE, MegaGate, Mega-mHC), DSpark, SWA bounded replay, both Engram placements. SGLang reproduced its benchmarks (lmsys.org/blog/2026-10-24-dsv41-deepinfer): "Up to 1.04x prefill throughput on 4x GB300 and 4x B300, up to 7.4% shorter decode steps on 64x GB300".
- Marlin (github.com/IST-DASLab/marlin): "FP16xINT4 LLM inference kernel that can achieve near-ideal ~4x speedups up to medium batchsizes of 16-32 tokens" - the reference for GEMV-class decode dequant; no published SM120 FP4 numbers found. Machete (vLLM csrc/quantization/machete) is Hopper-only. On Blackwell, FP4 tensor-core grouped GEMM (FlashInfer path) replaces both.
- Megakernel line: ForgeMegakernel (2609.12379) reports decode megakernels at "50.5-85.9% MBU" and "1.21x over SGLang 0.5.18"; Ada-MK (2605.11581): "kernel launch overhead alone can account for 14.6% of end-to-end inference time", single-batch "+23.6% over vanilla TensorRT-LLM and 50.2% over vLLM" (L20); One-Megakernel-Is-Not-Enough (2610.35336): composition beats monolith ("21.4% lower mean batch-p95 TTFA and 28% higher throughput than the evaluated monolith megakernel" at bs32); MegaFlux (2610.00671): pipelined expert replication "hides 56-76% of replica-weight transfer cost", 1.13-1.26x vLLM prefill on DeepSeek-V4-Pro (B200 x8); MoK (2609.36070) is NVL72-only.
- Absolute bytes/s on SM120 for FP4 MoE decode: not published anywhere found. Only MBU percentages (ForgeMegakernel) and relative speedups exist. Treat any "X GB/s on RTX PRO 6000" claim as UNVERIFIED.
- 2/3-bit experts: MoQE (2310.02410) "2-bit expert weights... model size... reduced by 79.6%... 1.24X speed-up on A100"; VPTQ (2409.17066), QTIP (2406.11235), QuIP# (2402.04396), AQLM (2401.06118) all demonstrate 2-3-bit PTQ with modest loss on dense models; CAMERA-Q (2508.02322) does 2-bit MoE with micro-expert structure. None ship Blackwell tensor-core 2-bit decode kernels; dequant-to-FP4 or LUT-GEMV-style CPU-style paths would be needed. No BitNet-style server-GPU kernel numbers found.
- Expert prefetch: moot - FP4 weights (~250GB class) fit in 4x96GB; offload systems (Fiddler 2402.07033, LeanStream 2609.03079) target smaller GPUs.

## 4. Reducing per-step bytes without quality loss

- Hard truth: for a fixed checkpoint, no published bytes-cut below its shipped precision is provably quality-neutral. The checkpoint already ships FP4 experts + FP8 dense + FP4 KV + inter-layer KV sharing ("pushing the global KV footprint to 890 bytes per token" - vLLM blog 2026-10-07).
- Activation/contextual sparsity is the only measured-near-neutral family: DejaVu (2310.17157) "speed up LLM inference in wall-clock time without compromising LLM's quality" (">2X" on OPT-175B). But BlockFFN (2507.08771) warns vanilla MoE "sparsely-activated architectures exhibit low chunk-level sparsity... incompatible with mainstream acceleration techniques (e.g., speculative decoding)" - and our whole lever #1-4 stack is speculative. BlockFFN's fix needs retraining (80% token-level / 70% 8-token chunk sparsity, "up to 3.67x" on end-side devices).
- Expert pruning: CAMERA-P (2508.02322) is training-free and strong at 20-60% pruning, but benchmark scores drop - not neutral. No "expert pruning with compensation" paper found that claims zero degradation on a V4-class model.
- Skip layers / early exit: LayerSkip (2404.16710) "speedups of up to 2.16x on summarization" but requires a checkpoint trained for early exit - not ours. SkipDecode-class methods degrade generation quality per their own evals.
- Weight sharing/low-rank at inference: nothing applicable found for a fixed 502B MoE (MobileLLM-style reuse is a training-time design).
- Head pruning: nothing quality-neutral found in 2025-2026 for this class.
- Engram table bytes: GateRQ (2610.35985) cuts Engram GPU memory "without retraining" via gate-ranked 1-bit residual ladders - the only strictly lossless-ish bytes lever found in this category (Engram loss is severe: "removing them from DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash costs 36.8 PopQA points").

## 5. Attention: sparse MLA / DSA / indexer / KV / Engram / P-D

- Native: V4.1-Flash uses CSA2 + FP4 KV + inter-layer KV sharing = "890 bytes per token" (vLLM blog). DSA itself: DeepSeek-V3.2 (2512.02556) "substantially reduces computational complexity while preserving model performance in long-context scenarios".
- Indexer is the decode bottleneck at long context: MLI (2611.03768): "every layer is preceded by a lightning indexer that scores the whole cache and keeps the k=2048 highest-scoring tokens... At long context this indexer scan, not the sparse attention it feeds, is where the decode time goes." Fix needs "no retraining and no new weights", runs "inside SGLang's production decode graph", "up to 14.5% faster on DeepSeek-V3.2... at 1/2 [retention]", "at 128K it reaches 1.32x the unmodified model's decode throughput at 1/4". Directly portable logic for V4.1 CSA2 on vLLM.
- StreamIndex (2605.02568): streaming top-k for the compressed indexer at V4-Flash dims; extends feasible S from 65,536 (OOM) to "S=1,048,576 with 6.21 GB peak HBM", recall "at least 0.9980" (bit-exact where both fit).
- Lookahead Sparse Attention (2606.09079): Neural Memory Indexer predicting future context demands (V4-based); research-stage.
- KV compression "lossless in practice": KeepKV (2504.09936) "single-step lossless compression and error bounds for multi-step"; KVTuner (2502.04420) "nearly lossless" layer-wise mixed precision; CLLA (2410.15252) reaches "lossless performance on most tasks" at <2% size but is an architecture change (inapplicable to a fixed ckpt). Marginal for us: KV is already 890 B/token.
- SWA: V4.1 CSA2 carries SWA; the serving trick is "SWA bounded replay" (vLLM/SGLang blogs) trading one window re-prefill per cache hit for cache space.
- Engram: DeepSeek's conditional n-gram memory in V4.1-Flash; serving placements verified in SGLang: host-memory tables (`SGLANG_ENABLE_DSV41_ENGRAM_HOST_TABLE=1`) or "split across the GPUs, and each GPU reads the rows it needs straight from its peers" (`SGLANG_OPT_DSV41_ENGRAM_PEER_SHARDS=1`) - the peer-read path is NVLink-motivated, so on PCIe expect it to underperform; prefer host placement + GateRQ quantization.
- P/D disaggregation: throughput/SLO tool (DistServe 2401.09670 "7.4x more requests"; Mooncake 2407.00079 "+525%... 75% more requests"). For a single stream it does not raise tok/s; skip.

## 6. Systems: graphs, persistent kernels, launch gaps, comm, overlap

- CUDA graph coverage: vLLM's V4.1-Flash work added "decoder CUDA graphs" over layers that previously "run eagerly" (21-39), contributing to a "1.9x speedup at low concurrency" (vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-07-deepseek-v41-flash). SGLang's DSpark path keys "the graph on the total token count... replays a genuinely smaller graph" for ragged per-request verify lengths (lmsys.org/blog/2026-07-06-dspark-sglang/).
- Launch overhead: measured at "14.6% of end-to-end inference time" for decode (Ada-MK, 2605.11581) - the justification for megakernels/persistent kernels; on PCIe TP4 the CPU launch stream is shared with comm, so this matters more.
- Fused all-reduce: vLLM: "We aggressively fused and parallelized the remaining kernels, including mHC side streams, and fused all-reduce with the preceding and following ops into a single kernel" (2026-10-07 post). FlashInfer ships custom AllReduce + fused RMSNorm ("AllReduce: Custom implementations; Normalization: RMSNorm... fused operations", README).
- No-NVLink TP4: vLLM custom allreduce kernels for small worlds: vllm/distributed/device_communicators/custom_all_reduce.py (one-shot paths need P2P over PCIe). NCCL knobs: NCCL_P2P_LEVEL / related envs documented at docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/user-guide/docs/env.html. No published 4x-RTX-PRO-6000 TP4 tuning numbers found.
- Persistent kernels: DeepGEMM PDL/low-latency line (SM90/100), megakernel family (above), Weights That Stay (2611.00506) for the serial draft walk: "keeps projection shards on chip for the entire serial draft walk... the DSpark walk is up to 11.6x faster".
- Stream-level/overlap scheduling: SGLang "zero-overhead scheduling... integrated into SGLang's overlap scheduler with almost no DSpark-specific special-casing" (DSpark blog); DeepInfer-era SGLang shows the same scheduling figure (dsv41-deepinfer blog).
- TensorFold (github.com/ashhart/TensorFold): "serves language models from a Zig binary on Apple Silicon and NVIDIA GPUs... runs Metal or CUDA kernels directly. Serving needs no Python" - real project, agent-contribution workflow (ATC). No published decode tok/s for Blackwell MoE; treat as watchlist. (Term "TensorFold" appears to refer to this engine; no paper found.)

## 7. Published >600 tok/s single-stream results?

- On 4x96GB (or any 4x RTX PRO 6000) with V3.x/V4.x: NO published result found (searched DDG/Bing, GitHub, HF; nothing credible). No DeepSeek-V3.x-era 4x96GB number close to 600 exists either.
- Verified closest datapoints (all NVL72 rack class, not 4x96GB):
  - vLLM blog 2026-10-23 (vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-23-deepseek-v41-flash-pd-wide-ep): "A specialized decode path reaches 826 tokens/s for a single user" - GB300 NVL72, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, P/D + Wide-EP context; decode-vs-DeepInfer runs at "131K context, nextn=3"; AgentX at "P90 of 196 tokens/s per user" and "954K tokens/s per GPU" aggregate.
  - SGLang vs DeepInfer (lmsys.org/blog/2026-10-24-dsv41-deepinfer): decode measured at 16-256 requests/GPU on 64x GB300 (EP64) - batched, no single-stream point published.
- DERIVED feasibility for our stack (arithmetic from published constants, NOT a measurement): TP4 gives 4 x 1.45 = 5.8 TB/s aggregate; ~16B active params/token (V4.1 paper) at FP4/FP8 effective ~4.5-6 bits/param => roughly 9-12 GB read/token => ~1.6-2.1 ms/step => ~480-630 tok/s bandwidth ceiling before PCIe comm (2 all-reduces/layer x 40 layers) and launch gaps. A drafter with ~3.5-4 mean accepted tokens per verify (levers 1-3) is therefore the plausible path above 600 tok/s; verify batches of 6-7 tokens keep the step in the memory-bound regime where this arithmetic holds. Measure; do not quote this as a benchmark.
