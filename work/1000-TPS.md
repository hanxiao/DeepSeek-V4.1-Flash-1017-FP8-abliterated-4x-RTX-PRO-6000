### 1.4 Why FLy is banned (the mechanism, for the record)

FLy accepts a draft token when the target's entropy is above a threshold and the next `window` drafts
would be natively accepted. The vLLM docs say it "does not preserve the target distribution". Observed
in-session: `100%` decoded as `500%`, `FLy` as `Flang` — semantically plausible neighbours, not garbage,
but a different distribution. Han's call: exact only.
(`/Volumes/han2tb/dsh-workspace/ds41-audit/fly-lossy.html` has the worked example.)

## 2. Measurements this round (exact block rejection, top_p 1.0, T=1.0 unless stated)

Codebench C1, the repo's coding-agent workload: /v1/messages, tools, thinking at max effort, 30k/120k
tokens of real sglang source context, 4096 output tokens, fresh session id per request. Per-arm medians.

### 2.1 The k sweep (block rejection, top_p 1.0)

| arm | k | rows | 30k tok/s | AL 30k | 120k tok/s | AL 120k | step ms (AL/rate) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| b5 | 5 | 6 | 313.2* | 3.40 | **365.8** | **3.31** | 9.05 |
| b7 | 7 | 8 | 365.1 | 3.61 | 345.2 | 3.51 | 10.17 |
| b15 | 15 | 16 | 191.9 | 2.92 | 183.4 | 2.81 | 15.32 |

\* the b5 30k arm ran on a container that had just finished graph capture; two of its four runs were
cold (243, 252 tok/s). Its 120k pair and its thinkbench (cb30 411 tok/s, 3.70 tok/step) are consistent
with the historical 3.40-3.45 / 377-386. Treat the 30k cell as noisy, the 120k cell as the arm's number.

Read it:
1. **The step model holds exactly**: 9.05 -> 10.17 -> 15.32 ms for 6 -> 8 -> 16 rows is
   `6.05 + 0.61*rows` to within 0.05 ms at every point. The marginal row really is 0.61 ms.
2. **The shipped drafter cannot use a wide block.** AL peaks at k=7 (3.61) and *collapses* to 2.92 at
   k=15: the block-5 checkpoint's drafter degrades its own first five drafts when the block is widened,
   so the extra rows are paid twice — worse drafts and more bytes. Wide-k is 100% a drafter-training
   problem, not an engine problem.

### 2.2 The greedy arm (T=0, exact) — measured, and it is NOT a lever

| arm | k | 30k tok/s | AL 30k | 120k tok/s | AL 120k | step ms |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| t0k5 (temperature 0.0) | 5 | 218.1* | 3.53 | 213.5* | 3.45 | 16.2* |
| t0k7 (temperature 0.0) | 7 | **386.3** | **3.64** | 342.8 | 3.47 | 9.42 |

Two findings, both against the expectation:
1. **Greedy does not raise accepted length on the agent workload.** AL 3.53 vs 3.40 at T=1.0. The Fraser
   benchmark's AL 4.8-5.45 at T=0 does not transfer: that is a single code-generation prompt with
   thinking off, and the thinking trace is what the drafter is bad at, not the sampling noise.
2. **T=0 is step-neutral at k=7** (9.42 ms vs 10.17 at T=1.0), so there is no general greedy penalty.
   The k=5 arm's 16.2 ms is a cold-container artifact of that one arm (its four runs are 241/212/223/124
   tok/s, all slow); do not read it as a T=0 cost.

So the cheapest-hypothesised lever is dead in the other direction than expected: **greedy is neutral**,
because the cap is the drafter's fidelity to the thinking-trace distribution, not the sampling noise.
AL 3.5-3.65 at either temperature is the shipped drafter's ceiling on this workload.

## 3. The arithmetic of 1000 exact

`tok/s = 1000 * AL / step_ms`, so **1000 tok/s <=> AL (tokens) = step (ms)**. Today 3.45 vs 9.1.

### 3.1 The two axes and their measured constants

| constant | value | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B (step at 1 row, all fixed costs) | **6.05 ms** | two-point fit, confirmed at k=15 this round |
| c (marginal verified row) | **0.61 ms** | same, ~85-100% routed-expert bytes |
| AL today (T=1.0, exact) | **3.31-3.45** | this round + RESULTS.md |
| AL at T=0 (Fraser, code, thinking off) | 4.8 (k=5) / 5.45 (k=7) | README |
| AL ceiling of the shipped drafter | ~3.7 at any k | §2.1 + the c-vector [0.812, 0.759, 0.730, 0.672, 0.725] |

### 3.2 What each axis can buy

| workstream | step or AL | resulting tok/s at today's other axis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| today | 9.1 ms, AL 3.45 | 377 |
| **step cuts only** (the 7 quality-neutral items in `quant/PLAN-1000.md`, -3.22 ms) | 5.88 ms | **587** |
| **2-bit routed experts** (-1.5 ms) | 7.6 ms | 454 |
| step cuts + 2-bit | **4.38 ms** | **788** |
| drafter to AL 4.5 (DFlash2-class, matched T=1.0 config) | AL 4.5 | 494 at 9.1 ms; **1,027 at 4.38 ms** |
| drafter to AL 5.5 + step cuts + 2-bit | AL 5.5 | 1,256 at 4.38 ms |
| laddered verification (rows paid ~ AL, not k+1) | -5 ms at k=15 | makes wide k affordable; +1.4-1.55x at k=15 |

**So the honest statement: 1000 exact = (2-bit experts + the 7 step cuts + AL ~4.5).** Each of the
three is independently useful, and none of them alone gets past ~590.

### 3.3 Why AL 4.5 is the number that matters, and where it comes from

Published, matched sampling config (thinking, T=1.0, top_p 0.95, **lossless** rejection), Qwen3.5-4B
per-request mean accepted length: MTP 4.54, DFlash 4.92, **DSpark 5.49**, **DFlash2 5.97**
([inco.ai/blog/dflash2](https://inco.ai/blog/dflash2/), verified this round). DFlash2 holds a **flat
~86% conditional acceptance to the last position**; our curve is 0.81 -> 0.73 and then decays, which is
why our AL saturates at 3.7 while DFlash2's saturates at ~7.

The gap is not the verifier, it is the drafter: **DFlash2's two mechanisms are both drafter-side and
both cheap** — a 2M-param adjacent-pair **path selector** over the top-16 candidates (+0.34 AL at T=0,
+0.47 at T=1, +0.6% cycle latency) and a 16.5M-param **two-tap dynamic convolution** (+0.7% latency),
together +1.05 AL for +1.3% latency. The engine already ships the machinery:
`vllm/v1/worker/gpu/spec_decode/dflash2/speculator.py` has `DFlash2Speculator` with a graph-captured
`_selector_walk_kernel` (verified in the r21 tree this round). So it is a `DSparkSpeculator` subclass
plus a 2M-param training run, not new kernels.

### 4.1 The cheap hypotheses that are now measured dead

- **Serve greedy (T=0).** Tested this round (§2.2): AL 3.64 at k=7 vs 3.61 at T=1.0 — neutral. The
  Fraser prompt's AL 4.8-5.45 at T=0 is a thinking-off code prompt, not this workload. Not a lever.
- **Wide k with the shipped drafter.** Tested: AL 2.81-2.92 at k=15, 183 tok/s. Not a lever until the
  drafter is retrained at a wide block.
- **k=7.** Re-measured: 365.1 / 345.2 tok/s vs k=5's 365.8 — neutral at 30k, worse at 120k.

### 4.2 What is left, in one line each

- 2-bit experts: -1.5 ms, 10-20 days, quality gate. **Highest EV, start it.**
- The 7 step cuts: -3.22 ms, all bit-exact, ~20 days. **Certain, parallel, start it.**
- Block-16 drafter + DFlash2 selector: AL 3.6 -> 4.5-5.5, 6 days + one 2-day training run. **The only
  thing that moves the AL axis; the last retrain failed because it stayed at block 5.**
- Laddered verification: only after the drafter has a long tail.

## 5. The verdict

- **1000 tok/s exact at T=1.0 is reachable, but it is a program, not a knob**: 2-bit experts + the step
  bundle + a block-16 drafter at the DFlash2 frontier, ~35-45 engineer-days, ~2 months solo, ~3 weeks
  with three people. Nothing in the published literature or in this repo's 66 rounds gets there in one
  move, and **nobody has shipped 1000 single-stream on this model family and this hardware** — the best
  public third-party number is 249.1 tok/s (local-inference-lab Karmic Kraken, 4x RTX PRO 6000 Max-Q),
  and the best number anywhere on this box is our own 377.7 exact / 463.5 FLy.
- **The realistic near-term is 600-800**: step cuts alone give 587, + 2-bit gives 788, + greedy sampling
  (if acceptable) gives ~1,030.
- The two things the last session got right and that still hold: the step model (0.61 ms/row, B 6.05)
  and "the MoE is at its DRAM floor, only a smaller expert format moves it". The thing it got wrong:
  spending two weeks on a drafter retrain at block 5, where the tail was already saturated.
